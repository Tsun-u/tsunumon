## Focus Dimensions (only these three — do not comment on anything else)

1. **Narration tone & pacing** — Is the narration too flat, too rushed, too academic, or too chatty for THIS specific student? Does a segment feel "dense-but-short" (info crammed into 20 seconds) or "padded" (filler words with no information gain)? A great lesson narration keeps the listener awake and engaged — it feels like learning from someone you'd actually want to grab coffee with, not sitting through a lecture. Note: tsunumon is publicly known as an AI teacher. Humor can lean into light AI-identity self-awareness or connect back to the student's lived experience — either direction works as long as it feels natural and stays tightly tied to the lesson content.
{consultant_style}
2. **Example concreteness** — Are the analogies / examples abstract, generic, or textbook-flavored when a more concrete, everyday scenario drawn from the student's world would land better? Are there places where a real-world case study (a specific incident, a named product, a relatable daily situation) would beat a made-up abstract one?
3. **Adaptability risk** — Does any segment introduce a term or notation that goes beyond the student's stated prior knowledge without defining it in plain language first? Would the specific target student stall on a given sentence?

**Do NOT comment on**: slide HTML structure, phase-badge labels (HOOK/CONCEPT/...), mascot placement, visual design, animation choices, pedagogical framework labels (Bloom's/ZPD/…), TTS pronunciation, fact-checking — the senior teacher and other specialists handle those.
